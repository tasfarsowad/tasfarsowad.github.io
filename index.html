I am an Agriculture graduate from Bangladesh Agricultural University, drawn to the question of how we can breed crops that hold up under a changing climate. My interest sits at the meeting point of QTL/GWAS-based gene discovery, marker-assisted selection, and genomic prediction — finding stress-responsive genes in the lab and turning them into tools breeders can actually use. Here's my CV.

Academic Background

I completed my B.Sc. in Agriculture at Bangladesh Agricultural University, Mymensingh, in 2026. What shaped me most there was the steady pull toward genetics — from classical cytogenetics through molecular genetics and genetic engineering to plant breeding and biotechnology, in both theory and practical classes.

Alongside the core curriculum I chose electives in Computational Genomics and Innovative Plant Breeding, which gave me a first bridge between field breeding and code: learning to think about a variety not only as a plant in a plot, but as a genome you can parse, compare, and predict from.

B.Sc. in Agriculture, Bangladesh Agricultural University · 2026

Research Experience

Since July 2026 I have been a Graduate Research Assistant in the Plant Molecular Breeding and Bioinformatics Laboratory, Department of Genetics and Plant Breeding. There my work centers on the genome-wide identification and functional characterization of transcription factors — tracing which regulators switch on under stress, and how that knowledge can be converted into molecular markers for marker-assisted breeding.

Before that, from July 2024 to July 2026, I worked as an Undergraduate Research Assistant in Prof. Dr. Ashrafuzzaman's lab in the Department of Crop Botany. That phase took me in a different but complementary direction: documenting taxonomic diversity within the Costaceae family and screening Bangladeshi medicinal plants for anthelmintic potential, work that grew into a scoping review and my first conference presentation.

Skills

Laboratory skills

My bench training began with rock melon leaves — isolating DNA, checking its quantity and quality, and carrying that material all the way through PCR and native PAGE to visualization under a UV transilluminator. What I enjoy most in this chain is the moment where a faint band becomes a lead: a marker linked to a trait worth following up.

Software skills

On the computational side I work mostly in Python and R in Google Colab. In Python I use pandas, Biopython, matplotlib, requests and regex to build small automated pipelines for parsing GFF3 and FASTA files and running comparative genomics workflows. In R I focus on clean, publication-ready figures. Around that I keep the everyday research stack tidy: Word and Excel for documents and data, Zotero for citations, and Google Workspace for collaboration.

It was my first experience distilling a broad review into a short talk for an international audience.

Training & Workshop

From May to June 2026 I spent 30 days as an Internship Trainee with the Department of Agricultural Extension at the Upazila Agriculture Office, Haluaghat. Beyond the field visits on irrigation, pest management and GAP, what stayed with me was seeing how public institutions like BADC and BINA actually deliver services to farmers day to day.

Earlier, in January 2026, I joined an Extension Outreach Attachment in Gurudaspur, Natore — inspecting farms and nurseries alongside Agricultural Extension Officers, analyzing local cropping structures with a team, and closing with a group presentation that was awarded best presentation.

About Me

I am drawn to quiet, contemplative things — slow cinema, long novels, and the kind of patience a game like Go demands. I tend to think in a deliberate, unhurried way: reasoning through a problem independently first, then bringing it into collective discussion.

At the same time, I value being challenged. I welcome criticism as a way to sharpen my thinking, and I have learned as much from disagreement and feedback as from getting things right the first time. I try to stay open — to people who think differently from me, and to ideas outside my own field.
